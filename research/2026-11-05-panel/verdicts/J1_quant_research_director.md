## Most decision-changing finding

B3's max-of-N calibration: the best of 200 noise rules exceeds t=1.96 in 99% of worlds; a preselected rule does so in 2.6%. It survives scrutiny because it is a property of search, not of markets. It reverses the default: every slot, filter or parameter that emerged from a Study 66-style search is unverified until the full trial matrix is reconstructed. If it cannot be, the honest state is "not distinguishable from search noise," and proposals 1, 2, 6 and 7 are then tested on slots whose existence may already be a selection artefact.

## Biggest identification/leakage risk nobody addressed

**Program-level multiplicity with no held-out period.** Each report pre-registers its own test at α≈0.05, but all ten proposals (≈3 variants each) will run on the same ~750 sessions, and the survivors will be deployed with no untouched data. This is Study 66's problem one level up. Fix, before any log is opened: freeze the most recent ~125 sessions (6 months); run every test on the remainder; evaluate the final assembled portfolio once on the holdout under conservative fills. Cost ~17% power per test; benefit one honest out-of-sample number for the whole program.

## What the director failed to ask

**"What is the minimum effect worth acting on, and which tests can detect it?"** No power budget exists. A1's pooled MDES is d≈0.31, B2's power at a 0.3R interaction is 34%, B4's W holds ~60 sessions, and proposal 4 (b)–(d) cannot be powered at all. Tests that cannot detect an economically meaningful effect should not run: a null would be misread as "no effect" and a positive is disproportionately noise. Decision changed: run order and run/no-run. Feasible test (one afternoon, trade log only): count unique sessions per cell per proposal, compute closed-form MDES at the realized session-level R dispersion, rank against a stated threshold (e.g., 0.1R/session net of costs); proposals above threshold default to "no change" without a test.
